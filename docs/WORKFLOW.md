# Research workflow

1. **Scope**: establish the question, boundaries, audience, dates and seeds.
2. **Retrieve**: expand terminology and follow references/citing work across relevant venues, journals and official projects.
3. **Screen and log**: deduplicate versions, screen relevance, capture source passages and limitations.
4. **Map**: connect problems, applications, groups, decision authority where relevant, and measured evidence.
5. **Explain history**: show what changed, which milestone addressed it, and what remained unsolved.
6. **Select the frontier**: use the five criteria and cover distinct mechanisms/settings without imposing a fixed paper count.
7. **Propose tests**: turn observed limits into questions, baselines, metrics and experiments.
8. **Author and verify**: adapt the retained design, render every slide, reconcile counts and export supported deliverables.

The full operational sequence lives in [the skill workflow](../skills/research-boost/references/survey-workflow.md). [Research statistics](../skills/research-boost/references/research-statistics.md) specifies the additional work required for quantitative claims.

## Five frontier criteria

| Criterion | Evidence to capture |
| --- | --- |
| Challenging problem | A consequential failure or bottleneck that strong existing approaches struggle with. |
| Elegant, usable idea | A clear mechanism and interface, with assumptions and dependencies. |
| Outperform strong baselines | Comparable tasks/budgets, meaningful results, ablations and trade-offs. |
| Works from leading groups | Verified group and contribution; prominence is context, never proof of quality. |
| Demonstrated scaling | What grows, tested range, quality/cost, and simulation versus deployment. |

These criteria guide selection rather than define a universal research-impact score. Record untested criteria explicitly and include important evaluation/adoption contributions with their evidence type identified.
