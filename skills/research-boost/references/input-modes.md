# Inputs and scope

Accept one or several seeds. Record the seed type and what it can establish. Default to a research audience new to the field, English, and a scoped orientation; fit slide count to the requested duration.

## Prefer quality materials already available

Encourage the user to provide strong papers, literature reviews or surveys, curated reading lists, relevant notes, course/talk materials, and official pages from research groups they already follow. Concrete sources help establish the intended problems, vocabulary, methods and community more clearly than keywords alone. Use keywords to expand discovery, or as the starting point when materials are unavailable.

Read accessible supplied materials first. Record their source type, date, scope and any user explanation of why they matter. Treat reviews and notes as orientation; verify research claims against primary work. Follow references, citing work and neighboring groups to include missing or competing approaches. Do not assume that a supplied collection is complete or that trust in its source validates every claim. Log inaccessible inputs without claiming to have read them.

## Papers and research materials

Read supplied papers, bibliographies and related materials. Extract the actual problem, mechanism, assumptions, comparisons and limitations. Resolve DOI/arXiv/version for research works and trace claims from secondary materials to those works. Follow references backward for history and citing work forward for developments. Include contradictory and alternative approaches. A single paper is a seed, not a field census.

## Keywords

Expand vocabulary with synonyms, historical terminology, mechanism names and application settings. Write inclusion and exclusion rules. Search relevant general and specialist venues, journals and official projects. Log queries and the fields searched. Screen false positives and missed seeds before counting.

## Researcher

Use an official profile, institutional page, ORCID or verified publication list to resolve identity. Ask for a link if names remain ambiguous. Separate affiliations at publication time from current affiliation. Group contributions by problem and mechanism, follow coauthors/references/citing work, and include other schools of thought. Do not infer contribution or endorsement from coauthorship alone.

## Research group

Resolve the lab/team from its official website. Record public papers, projects, releases and datasets, with dates. Distinguish a public paper from open code, data or weights; classify the contribution, not the institution. Expand to neighboring and competing groups. A lab-centered input can produce a lab profile if requested; otherwise keep the output about the field.

## Scope record

Save topic, question, seed types/values, audience, duration, language, years, cutoff, exclusions, required deliverables and optional presenter wording. Mark inferred choices. Do not silently invent papers or person identities. If a seed is inaccessible, log it and continue with accessible evidence without claiming to have read it.
